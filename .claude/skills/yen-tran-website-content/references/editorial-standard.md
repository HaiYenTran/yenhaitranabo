# Editorial standard

## Voice

- Write in natural Vietnamese: warm, direct, calm, and credible.
- Sound like a thoughtful adviser sharing something useful, not a salesperson performing enthusiasm.
- Prefer concrete verbs, short paragraphs, everyday examples, and honest limitations.
- Avoid inflated promises, fear-based persuasion, vague inspiration, excessive exclamation marks, all-caps copy, and emoji decoration.
- Address the reader as `bạn`; use `Yến` or `mình` only when the page's established voice calls for it.

## Recommended article shape

Adapt the structure to the subject; do not force every heading when it adds no value.

1. A title that states the real question or benefit.
2. A short opening that names the reader's situation.
3. The useful answer early, followed by explanation and evidence.
4. Practical steps, examples, or a tool the reader can use.
5. A short summary or reflection.
6. Exactly one next-step CTA aligned with the site's journey.

## SEO fields

- One unique, human-readable `<title>`; put the primary topic early when natural.
- One concise description that accurately previews the page and gives a reason to click.
- One visible H1. Use H2/H3 as an information hierarchy, not for visual styling.
- Use the primary phrase naturally in the title, H1, opening, and at least one useful subheading when appropriate. Never chase a keyword density target.
- Use descriptive anchors; avoid repeated `xem thêm` links without context.
- Canonical must point to the official `https://yenhaitran.com/...` URL.
- Open Graph title, description, URL, image, and image alt must match the page.
- Use `Article`, `Book`, `Event`, `BreadcrumbList`, or another supported schema only when the visible page genuinely supplies the required information.

## Internal linking

- Link upward to the one primary group.
- Link to two or three genuinely useful supporting pages when available.
- Supporting articles link back to their pillar page with descriptive language.
- Do not create circular CTA chains or make the contact page the only exit from every paragraph.

## Evidence presentation

- Attribute important claims close to the claim and include usable source links.
- Translate research into plain language and state uncertainty where relevant.
- Personal stories may illustrate an idea but must not be presented as proof of general effectiveness.
- For health content, distinguish general education from individual diagnosis or treatment.

## Final review

- Does the page answer the query without requiring a second article?
- Is its primary keyword already owned by another official page?
- Can a mobile reader understand the first screen quickly?
- Is the next step obvious and singular?
- Are all factual claims supportable and all source links working?
- Does the page preserve the reader's autonomy and trust?
